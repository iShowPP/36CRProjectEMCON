Scam chain detector

Builds the path that led to a page from link clicks, redirects and tabs opened by other tabs.

  1. Click a real short link: youtu.be/jNQXAC9IVRw or aka.ms/terminal.
  2. On the page it lands on, open EMCON → This site.
  3. Or paste an SMS with a short link into EMCON → Check message.
You should seeHow you got here: this page → the short link → the HTTP redirect → the landing page. With no scam wording and no request for card details, it stays a note, not a warning. On a phishing site, the full chain joins the block evidence, showing how the message led to the fake form.
04

Content Credentials on real photos

The official sample files of the C2PA standards body, on GitHub: a real Truepic camera capture, plus files the C2PA team broke on purpose to test verifiers. EMCON checks the signature, every assertion, the image hash and the signer's certificate against the official C2PA trust list.

  1. Wait a few seconds for the labels (the Truepic file is 2 MB), then click one for the evidence. Each image opens on its own GitHub page, with the same result there.
  2. On the Truepic photo, click the label → Recognise copies. The EMCON server downloads the file itself, re-checks the credentials and keeps only the photo's visual fingerprint. The stripped CDN copy then shows Recovered · Truepic for everyone using EMCON.
  3. Compare with the official verifier: Truepic, edited, broken signature. The C2PA test file list says what is wrong with each file.
  4. For your own photos: in the dashboard → Photo signing, prove you own your website (a file at /.well-known/emcon-publisher.txt or a DNS TXT record), then sign photos there. Anyone with EMCON sees Verified · yourdomain.com on them.
You should seeThe labels in the captions, the same verdicts as the official verifier. The evidence lists the signer, the certificate issuer, the signing time and each check. Images with no credentials get no label at all: missing provenance proves nothing.
05

Privacy promise monitor

EMCON sends Global Privacy Control, a "do not sell or share my data" signal the site actually receives, then watches what the page does.

  1. Open a big news site and scroll a little:
timesofindia.indiatimes.com edition.cnn.com forbes.com wired.com theguardian.com
  1. Open EMCON → Privacy.
  2. Check the signal is really sent: globalprivacycontrol.org says "GPC signal detected", and httpbin.org/headers shows Sec-Gpc: 1.
  3. If a site shows you a cookie banner, click its Reject all. EMCON notices and tests "stops tracking after you say no" as well. Most sites only show the banner to visitors from Europe.
You should seeIn our tests from India: Times of India, CNN, Forbes and Wired loaded 43 to 48 known tracking domains each despite the signal, so Honours your "do not sell or share" signal is Broken, with every domain named. The Guardian loaded none, so Kept. GPC is a legal opt-out in California, Colorado and several other US states; elsewhere it is a request, and the row says so.
06

Anti-fingerprinting

Runs before the page's first script, so even fingerprinting code at the very top of a page sees the noise.

  1. Open browserleaks.com/canvas and note the Signature. Reload: it changes, so visits can't be linked.
  2. EMCON → More → Settings → Pause fingerprint protection here, reload: you see your real signature, the same on every reload.
  3. Open browserleaks.com/webgl: Unmasked Renderer shows a common graphics card instead of yours.
  4. See who fingerprints you: on stackoverflow.com or economist.com, EMCON → Privacy lists the canvas, audio and font probing.
You should seeA new signature on every visit with EMCON on, and the real, stable one with it off for that site. Pages keep working: screen size, language and time zone are never faked.
07

Payment brake and family

Scammers rush people. When EMCON blocks a scam page that asks for card, OTP, PIN or bank details, continuing takes 5 minutes, or one tap to ask a family member first.

  1. Sign in to the dashboard → Browsers & family and click Connect a browser. In the browser you want to protect: EMCON icon → More → Settings → EMCON account, enter the 6-digit code → Connect.
  2. In that browser (a separate profile, never type anything), open a live phishing site from demo 02 that asks for card or OTP details. Many in the feed do: bank, card and parcel-fee scams.
  3. The red block page shows Need to pay here anyway?: Continue in 5:00 counts down, and Ask <your name> to check it sends the request to you.
  4. On your dashboard, Waiting for you lists the site and EMCON's reasons. Click Stop it or It's fine.
You should seeThe protected browser updates within seconds: Blocked by your family, or Continue unlocked. The countdown survives a reload. Pages that only ask for a password keep the normal "continue anyway" link: the brake is for money. The dashboard shows counts and these requests, never browsing history.
08

Checkout script-change alarm

"Magecart" skimmers hide a script on a real shop's payment page that reads your card as you type. Reputation checks can't catch it: the shop is genuine. EMCON remembers which outside scripts a payment page loads and warns when a new one appears.

  1. Open a real page with a card form: W3Schools' checkout form (card fields in the page, about 20 scripts, many from ad networks) or the EFF's donation checkout (Stripe's card field). EMCON → This site → Payment page scripts: "First visit to this payment page: EMCON recorded its scripts". Everything the page loads during this first visit counts as normal.
  2. Close the tab and open the page again: The same scripts as on your first visit.
  3. Now do what a skimmer does on a hacked shop. Press F12 → Console, paste and press Enter:
    document.head.append(Object.assign(document.createElement('script'), { src: 'https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/jquery@3.7.1/dist/jquery.min.js' }))
  4. Click the Card number field.
You should seeAn amber bar before you type: A script from cdn.jsdelivr.net appeared on this payment page since your visit… Don't enter card details unless you know why it changed. It comes back when you click a card field again. Trust this change accepts it as the new normal. Changed content of known scripts, new files from the shop itself and new scripts from known ad or analytics services are notes, not alarms: shops update constantly.
09

Crowdsourced website ratings

Star ratings and tags from real people, signed by each person's EMCON key. Only verified people count: one blind Ghost token proves you're a person without saying who.

  1. Open a site you have used, for example amazon.in or flipkart.com.
  2. EMCON → This site → Rate it: pick stars and up to 4 tags (e.g. delivered as promised, fake product), then Submit rating.
  3. With Ghost set up (demo 11) the rating counts as a verified person. Without it, it is saved and signed but shown as unverified.
You should seeThe average from verified people, the most used tags, and ratings from people you trust by name. Lookups send a 5-character hash prefix, not the site address, so the server doesn't learn which sites you open.
10

Trust by QR code

Scam warnings from people you know beat anonymous reviews. Swap QR codes in person once; after that their signed vouches and scam reports count for you.

  1. On two computers (or two browser profiles): EMCON → More → Share trust by QR code. Each shows a contact code; scan the other's with the webcam, or copy and paste the text code.
  2. On a real site, one of you clicks EMCON → This site → I used it safely (or Report scam) → Share it as a QR code. The other scans it.
  3. Open the same site on the other computer → This site.
You should seeUnder Scam and phishing reports: "Vouched for by 1 source in your web of trust", with their name (or their scam report). Codes are signed and expire after 90 days. A code from someone who isn't your contact is shown but not counted.
11

Ghost sign-in

Prove you're a real adult without giving a site your email, phone or name. Each site sees a different pseudonym, so sites can't link you.

  1. EMCON → More → Ghost sign-in → Set up Ghost. Verify once and choose a PIN (or passkey). EMCON receives blind humanity tokens.
  2. Open EMCON's login page and click Continue with EMCON Ghost. Approve in the EMCON window with your PIN.
You should seeYou are signed in to the dashboard with no email or password. The server checks the token's signature but can't tell who you are; the next visit recognises the same pseudonym without spending another token. Other sites can add the same button with the EMCON Ghost API.
